I am a data scientist who takes on complex, unfamiliar problems and moves them
forward. In research, that has meant new methodology; in business, it means
pragmatic solutions that get a decision made. At The Hershey Company, I own the
counterfactual predictions behind a production assortment planning
application. It pairs rich consumer choice models of how shoppers assemble
their baskets, fit to store-by-week-by-item point-of-sale data, with a
tractable optimization. The assortment planning literature generally favors
simple choice models, so this combination is new. It answers the question the
business cares about most (what happens if I add or remove items?) while
optimizing fast enough to iterate quickly on strategy.

My strengths are in applied statistical modeling, with deep expertise in
**discrete outcome models** (the models behind consumer choice and survey
data) and spatial statistics (models for data tied to locations). I work best
alongside the people who make the decisions, and I bring:

- **Communication with business stakeholders.** I translate technical details
  into the language of the people who use my models, so they understand what
  modeling choices mean for their business.
- **Alignment on the real question.** It is easy to build a precise model for
  the wrong question. Finding the mathematical formulation of what the
  business actually needs answered takes patience and real collaboration,
  including respect for stakeholders' subject-matter expertise.
- **Honesty about what the data can and cannot answer.** Once we agree on the
  question, I build trust by being clear about how well we can answer it. I
  direct data engineering work to build the views of data the problem needs,
  and I involve stakeholders throughout model development so they understand
  what the evidence supports, where the uncertainty lies, and the pragmatic
  path forward.
- **Direction set by consensus.** Together, these let me steer project
  priorities toward the most valuable, pragmatic solutions by building
  agreement across data science, business stakeholders, and product.

I completed my Ph.D. in Statistics and Data Sciences at the University of
Texas at Austin and held an NSF Mathematical Sciences Postdoctoral Research
Fellowship at Duke University. My dissertation was a finalist for the 2025 ISBA
Leonard J. Savage Award in the Applied Methodology category. I'm interested in
industry data science roles where careful modeling meets real business
decisions. See [Research](/research/) for the methodological side of my work.
