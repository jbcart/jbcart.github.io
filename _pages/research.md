My research combines statistical inference with optimization to support
decision-making under uncertainty, especially in retail and social-science
settings.

**Consumer choice & latent variable modeling.** I develop Bayesian models of
individual choice behavior — for example, how shoppers assemble a basket of
items — treating unobserved preferences as latent variables inferred from
high-dimensional behavioral data (shopping carts, voting records, survey
responses). I'm particularly interested in identifiable models, where distinct
parameter sets correspond to distinct probability models, since identifiability
improves the interpretability and reliability of inference in these settings.

**Statistics meets optimization.** Inference is rarely the end goal — it feeds
a decision. My current work at Hershey pairs consumer choice models with
tractable optimization procedures for assortment planning, and I'm broadly
interested in this interface: designing statistical models so that the
decisions built on top of them stay computationally tractable.

**Bayesian workflow.** I rely on the Bayesian workflow (prior elicitation,
model specification, prior/posterior predictive checks; [Gelman et al.
2020](http://www.stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/research/unpublished/Bayesian_Workflow_article.pdf))
as a principled way to connect domain expertise to downstream analysis and
decisions.

I remain interested in spatial statistics and functional data analysis from my
dissertation work, and in future applications of these ideas to social science
methodology.
